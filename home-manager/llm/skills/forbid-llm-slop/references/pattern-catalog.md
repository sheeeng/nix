# AI Slop Pattern Catalog

Use this catalog to diagnose clusters. Treat each entry as a signal, not a
sentence level ban. The correct fix is the shortest truthful rewrite that
preserves the source.

## Content and Evidence

* Significance inflation turns an ordinary fact into proof of a broad trend,
  legacy, or pivotal moment. State what happened and cut the ceremony.
* Notability name dropping lists publications, followers, or credentials
  without explaining what they establish. Keep the source that carries context.
* Superficial participle analysis appends words such as `highlighting`,
  `underscoring`, `reflecting`, `symbolizing`, `fostering`, or `showcasing`.
  Delete the tail or make its real information a sourced sentence.
* Promotional language uses travel brochure adjectives or product praise where
  facts would be stronger. Replace `vibrant`, `world class`, `groundbreaking`,
  `stunning`, `seamless`, and `state of the art` with evidence.
* Vague attribution assigns a claim to experts, observers, critics, reports,
  publications, or research without naming the source. Name it or remove it.
* Formulaic challenge sections use a good news sentence, a generic obstacle,
  and an optimistic outlook. State the specific problem, evidence, owner, and
  consequence, or remove the section.
* Vague declaratives announce that the stakes, implications, or reasons are
  significant without naming the consequence. State the consequence.
* False agency gives human actions to data, markets, decisions, complaints,
  cultures, or systems. Name the person or team that acted.
* Lazy extremes use `every`, `always`, `never`, `everyone`, or `nobody` where
  the source supports only a smaller claim. Use the observed scope.
* Evidence laundering turns a vague benefit into a polished claim. Preserve
  the proof gap instead of inventing a mechanism or result.

## Language and Sentence Structure

* Binary contrast frames the point as `not X but Y`, `the question is not X`,
  or `it is not just X`. State Y directly.
* Negative listing repeats `not X` and `not Y` before revealing Z. State Z.
* Fake alternatives introduce an option no reader raised, then reject it.
  State the actual constraint.
* Rhetorical setups ask a question, announce a reveal, or use `here is the
  thing`, `what nobody tells you`, `think about it`, or `plot twist`. Make the
  claim without the stage direction.
* Colon reveals put a dramatic lowercase answer after a noun phrase and colon.
  Keep colons for real lists, labels, and explanations.
* Anaphora abuse starts several sentences with the same subject or phrase.
  Combine related claims or vary the opening naturally.
* Tricolon abuse forces abstract ideas into three items. Use the number the
  content requires.
* Synonym cycling renames one subject in each sentence. Repeat the clearest
  term.
* False ranges use `from X to Y` when the endpoints do not form a real scale.
  List the actual items or describe the genuine range.
* Narrator distance uses `people tend to`, `nobody designed this`, or a lecture
  voice that avoids the scene. Put the reader, actor, or object in the sentence.
* Passive voice and subjectless fragments hide the actor. Use active voice when
  the actor is known and relevant.
* Listicles in prose turn each paragraph into `first`, `second`, and `third`
  takeaways. Use a real list or write connected prose.
* Repeated structure gives every paragraph the same topic, explanation,
  example, and transition shape. Vary the treatment when the content differs.
* One point dilution repeats one claim with new synonyms or examples. State it
  once, support it, and move on.
* Historical analogy stacking name drops several comparisons instead of
  examining one. Use one relevant example or none.
* Dead metaphors and invented concept labels make ordinary claims sound like a
  framework. Use the literal claim unless the metaphor does real work.

## Tone and Filler

* Chatbot residue includes `of course`, `certainly`, `great question`, `I hope
  this helps`, `would you like`, `let me know`, and offers to continue. Delete
  it from deliverable prose.
* Sycophancy praises the user before answering. Answer first.
* Acknowledgment loops restate the user's request before answering. Skip the
  restatement.
* Knowledge disclaimers mention model limits, training dates, or missing
  searches before making a guess. State what the source does not establish.
* Reasoning artifacts expose `let me think`, `breaking this down`, or internal
  step labels. Keep the conclusion and evidence, not private scaffolding.
* Filler includes `in order to`, `due to the fact that`, `at this point in time`,
  `in the event that`, `it is important to note`, and `has the ability to`.
  Prefer `to`, `because`, `now`, `if`, direct claims, and `can`.
* Adverb piles use `really`, `just`, `literally`, `honestly`, `simply`,
  `actually`, `truly`, `fundamentally`, or `inherently` without changing the
  claim. Keep one only when it carries voice or meaning.
* Hedging seesaws stack `could`, `potentially`, `possibly`, `arguably`, and
  `might`. Keep the uncertainty the evidence requires.
* Generic positive endings use `the future looks bright`, `exciting times`,
  `a journey`, or `the possibilities are endless`. End on a fact or action.
* Corporate pep talk uses `empower`, `unlock`, `elevate`, `streamline`,
  `supercharge`, `move the needle`, and `bridge the gap`. Name the action.
* AI vocabulary clusters around `delve`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `foster`,
  `robust`, `seamless`, `tapestry`, `realm`, `landscape`, `multifaceted`,
  `transformative`, `paramount`, `intricate`, `harness`, `synergy`, and
  `ever evolving`. Replace only when the word adds no precise meaning.
* Copula avoidance replaces `is`, `are`, and `has` with `serves as`, `stands as`,
  `marks`, `represents`, `boasts`, `features`, or `offers`. Use the simple verb.

## Formatting and Cadence

* Uniform sentence length makes prose sound metronomic. Mix short and long
  sentences when the thought supports it.
* Uniform paragraph length makes every idea look templated. Let paragraph size
  follow the idea.
* Parataxis chains short declarative fragments without showing how the ideas
  relate. Join related thoughts with syntax that expresses cause, contrast, or
  qualification.
* Manufactured punchlines make every sentence sound quotable. Keep emphasis
  occasional and let some paragraphs end plainly.
* Boldface overload, emoji decoration, title case headings, curly quotes,
  nested bullets, and inline bold labels make formatting perform importance.
  Use the medium's natural format.
* A heading restatement repeats the heading in the first sentence. Begin with
  the content.
* Excessive signposting uses `first`, `second`, `finally`, or `in this section`
  when the passage is short enough to read directly.
* Hyphenated pair overload applies compounds such as `high quality` and
  `real time` with a hyphen everywhere. Keep a hyphen only where grammar or a
  standard term requires it.
* Curly quotation and dash changes can be valid house style. Treat them as
  clusters unless the user requests a strict punctuation rule.

## Humanizer Signals

Preserve burstiness, vocabulary that belongs to the writer, concrete sensory
detail, local references, unresolved feelings, slang, deliberate fragments,
and the occasional awkward sentence that carries personality. Do not inject
random mistakes or odd words to imitate a detector's preferred statistics.

Useful review signals include sentence length variation, paragraph variation,
repeated trigrams, vocabulary diversity, density of unsupported abstractions,
and the number of claims that survive a summary. These signals support human
judgment. They do not prove authorship or quality.
